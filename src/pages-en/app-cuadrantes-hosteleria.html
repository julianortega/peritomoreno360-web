Staff is the other big cost

In hospitality, staff costs and food costs are the two expenses that decide whether a venue makes or loses money. A rota done by eye, with too many people on a Tuesday afternoon and too few on a Friday night, costs money and makes service worse.

That's why the rota has to be built around forecast sales, with the team cost in front of you before you publish it, not at the end of the month.

Working time records

Mandatory clock-in, without paperwork

Since 2019, companies in Spain have been required to record each employee's daily start and finish times and keep those records for four years. In hospitality, with split shifts and changing hours, doing it on paper is a constant source of errors.

With Flight Director each person clocks in at the venue or, if you enable it, from their own phone, and the record is saved alongside the rota, so you can compare planned hours with hours worked.

Frequently asked questions

Is recording working hours mandatory in hospitality?

Yes. Since May 2019 all companies in Spain must record each employee's start and finish times and keep the records for four years.

How do employees clock in?

At the venue itself or, if you prefer, from their phone by logging into Flight Director. When they clock in, the record is saved with the exact time.

Can I see staff costs before publishing the rota?

Yes. Each day on the rota shows the planned hours and their cost, as well as the forecast orders.

Does it work for several locations?

Yes. Each location has its own rota and you can see them all from the same account.
