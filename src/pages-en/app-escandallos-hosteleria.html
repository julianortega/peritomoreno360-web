What recipe costing is

A recipe costing (in Spain, an «escandallo») is the sheet that breaks down everything that goes into a dish and what it costs: each ingredient, its quantity, its price and, at the end, the cost per portion. With that figure you set the selling price and know how much each dish leaves you.

The problem is keeping them up to date. Supplier prices change every week, recipes evolve and for delivery you have to subtract the platform commission. A spreadsheet forgotten in a folder stops being useful within a few months. That's why we build them inside the app the kitchen uses every day.

What Flight Director does with your recipe costs

01

Cost to the cent

Ingredients and sub-recipes (sauces, bases, marinades) with their cost per kilo, unit or portion.

02

Prices always up to date

Change an ingredient's price and every dish that uses it is recalculated, without touching each costing.

03

Dine-in and delivery separately

The same dish with two calculations: VAT and no commission for dine-in; VAT and platform commission for delivery.

04

Error warnings

If an ingredient is missing a price, the app warns you that the real food cost is higher than it looks.

05

Several brands, one kitchen

The same dish can be sold under several brands or locations, each at its own price.

06

Kitchen spec sheets

A printable spec sheet with quantities and allergens so the whole team makes the dish the same way.

An example with numbers

A burrito sold for €12.90 dine-in includes 10% VAT, so the net price is €11.73. If the ingredients cost €2.84, the food cost is 24% and the gross margin is €8.89.

The same burrito at €14.90 on a delivery platform with a 20% commission leaves a net price of €13.55, of which €2.98 goes on commission. Subtracting the same €2.84 cost, the margin ends up at €7.73: more than a euro less than dine-in, even though the price is two euros higher. Without costing per channel, you can't see that difference.

Frequently asked questions

What is recipe costing in hospitality?

It's the sheet that calculates the cost of a dish from its ingredients and quantities. It's used to set the selling price and know the margin on each dish.

How do you calculate the cost of a dish?

Multiply the quantity of each ingredient by its price (allowing for trimming or cooking loss) and add them all up. The result is the cost per portion.

Does Flight Director account for delivery commissions?

Yes. Each dish shows the dine-in and delivery calculations separately, with the platform commission deducted.

Does it work for several locations?

Yes. Flight Director is multi-location and multi-brand: you can share recipes between locations and have different prices at each one.
